# Lesson 02 - Algebraic Identities and Factorisation

<span class="nmao-badge nmao-study">LESSON</span>

## Objective

Recognise standard algebraic patterns, expand expressions safely, and factor expressions to reveal hidden structure.

## 1. Essential identities

### Squares

$$
(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2,
$$

$$
(a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2.
$$

### Difference of squares

$$
a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b).
$$

### Cubes

$$
a^3-b^3=(a-b)(a^2+ab+b^2),
$$

$$
a^3+b^3=(a+b)(a^2-ab+b^2).
$$

### Three-variable square

$$
(a+b+c)^2
=a^2+b^2+c^2+2(ab+bc+ca).
$$

## 2. Why factorisation matters

Factorisation converts a sum into a product. Products reveal:

- when an expression is zero;
- when an expression is positive or negative;
- useful cancellations;
- hidden squares and equality cases.

For example:

$$
x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3).
$$

So $x^2-9=0$ becomes

$$
(x-3)(x+3)=0,
$$

which gives $x=3$ or $x=-3$.

## 3. Main factorisation patterns

### Common factor

$$
6x^2+9x=3x(2x+3).
$$

### Grouping

$$
ax+ay+bx+by
=a(x+y)+b(x+y)
=(a+b)(x+y).
$$

### Difference of squares

$$
4x^2-25y^2=(2x-5y)(2x+5y).
$$

### Perfect square

$$
x^2+6x+9=(x+3)^2.
$$

### Substitution

For an expression involving $x^4$ and $x^2$, set $u=x^2$. For example:

$$
x^4-5x^2+4
=u^2-5u+4
=(u-1)(u-4)
=(x^2-1)(x^2-4).
$$

Then factor again:

$$
x^4-5x^2+4
=(x-1)(x+1)(x-2)(x+2).
$$

## 4. Worked example

Factor

$$
x^3-8.
$$

Recognise a difference of cubes:

$$
x^3-8=x^3-2^3.
$$

Using $a^3-b^3=(a-b)(a^2+ab+b^2)$:

$$
x^3-8=(x-2)(x^2+2x+4).
$$

## 5. Safe algebra habits

- Expand slowly and check the middle term.
- Factor out a common factor first.
- Look for a difference of squares before using a longer method.
- If you substitute $u=x^2$, substitute back at the end.
- Never cancel a factor unless you know it is nonzero.
- Multiply your factors back together to verify.
